# Personal Journal
Everyone possesses a world within. One's beliefs, values and mental model give it shape. The world within is a land of chaos, mayhem and fluidity. It's the exact opposite to the world without – the land of strictness and rigour. 

The world within bears numerous ideas every moment, gorgeous ideas with the potential to reshape the whole landscape of the world within. But they perish into nothingness as if they never existed. The land of chaos shows no mercy. The intractable dynamic land of chaos.

One's eyes, one's speech, one's writing are the threads connecting these two lands of immiscible nature – the world within and the world without. And these connections are the only way for residents of the world within to prove their existence. If a resident of the world within – e.g. a value, a belief – doesn't manifest itself externally in the world without, it will perish into nothingness. There won't even be ashes. A change to your world within that doesn't manifest itself externally simply doesn't exist. That's why I always try to capture my thoughts in the world without. If I have time I'll write about them in my personal journal. I'll discuss them with my friends. I'll record voice messages(lately I prefer to not do it) or at least I'll chat about it. All these cases are examples of my struggles to reach an external manifestation of my world within, so it can exist.

# An Extract From my Journal
Levent Alpöge announced a proof of the Generalised Riemann Hypothesis(GRH) on 20th November 2025. It's funny that I first found out about this through a random reddit [post](https://www.reddit.com/r/mathmemes/comments/1y16a86/so_whats_gonna_be_new_goalpost_guyss/) on r/mathmemes. A couple days before GRH proof got released I wrote something in my personal journal which I think you'll find interesting. I was writing it in my personal journal, so I never meant to publish it in its raw form, that's why it may sound more informal and a bit harder to understand than other articles on parsalogue. 

> People forget what LLMs are. Their greatest aptitude shows itself in their names. They're Large Language Models. Obviously they should excel at analysing languages. I'm surprised that very few people have focused or discussed this aspect of LLMs. You know what would I do if I were an economist? I'd define certain indices which were based on News, then investigate the causality these indices bear on stocks price. A couple years ago such a task was impossible to do. But now you can analyse all the economical, political, sociological, hell even news about news toilets opening up in your neighbourhood. It's idiotic how everybody focuses on Generative aspects of LLMs and not their analytical aspects. I'm more baffled by how everybody's forcing LLMs to function as world models. Yet they're not using LLMs to analyse the exact thing they were fabricated to model, languages. 
> 
> LLMs don't work based on the scientific method. Machine Learning is fundamentally ill-posed. They can't implicitly find causality, at least for now. I, as the computer scientist, must malleate the neural network's structure so it can resemble the real world. 
> 
> The matter of formal sciences, however, is different than social and natural sciences. Formal sciences aren't based on the real world. Maths is a language. So you mustn't be surprised if LLMs solve maths(whatever that's supposed mean) before they scratch the surface of social and natural sciences. Let this be a evidence of your opinion(armstrong, ye affected me). 

I'm still amazed by GRH proof. Even though I predicted it would happen – well I haven't really predicted it. Any sensible human would tell you it's going to happen eventually – I'm still shocked by how fast it came to be. The "proof game" had been going on a while before GRH proof. Take Forced Navier-Stokes blow up solution as an example (announced 8th September 2026). It was the first AI-assisted proof (I'm not sure to what degree it was AI-assisted vs pure AI. But I know I couldn't have found the counter-example myself even if I had access to the strongest LLMs in existence. So the human certainly played a role) that went main stream. Navier-Stokes is one of the seven millennium problems announced by clay institute. That's why its solution attracted so much attention. It had been 24 years since one of these millennium problems were solved. Yet I wasn't that amazed when I heard about it, because it was a counter-example (well I was sad to a certain degree. Because Navier-Stokes was the only millennium problem related to me somehow. The other millennium problems are voodoo). It was trivial to me that LLMs must be much more capable than humans in coming up with counter-examples. A human must construct an elegant method to find out the viable counter-example candidates, a LLM is patient, hard-working and doesn't care about elegance unlike our fellow lazy humans (laziness invokes elegance).
